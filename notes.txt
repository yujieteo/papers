https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.26269, Scholze's paper on six functor formalism. Key definitions include: (1) Corr(C,E) to Cat_infty as a lax symmetric monoidal functor with right adjoints to projection formula, resulting in proper base change; (2) straightening and unstraighening resulting in the definition of a symmetric monoidal infinity category. Computations include: (1) Kunneth proof on page 8 to help define projection formula; (2) trivial Kan fibrations to define infinity categories on page 14; (3) propositional definitions 3.11 and 3.12 for Mann's definition resulting in a computation of the projection formula on pages 23 and 24;

https://mgallauer.warwick.ac.uk/docs/m6ff.pdf, Gallauer's six functor formalisms. Key definitions: (1) cohomology and homology in terms of six functors on the tensor unit with a dictionary in page 8; (2) Grothendieck duality dictionary on page 10; (3) relative purity for smoothness on page 11; (4) definition of localisation on page 15; (5) definition of proper and smooth base change on page 16; (6) definition of coefficient systems and axioms on page 18. Computations: (1) computation of a smooth and blow up triangle on page 16; (2) Betti numbers as an example on page 3; (3) examples of coefficient systems on page 8;

https://people.mpim-bonn.mpg.de/scholze/Gestalten.pdf, Scholze's paper on Gestalten or the opposite category of Stefanich rings. Key definitions: (1) from pages 11 to 12 the buildup to the definition of a Stefanich ring is given. Key examples: (1) examples of Tannakian duality are given on page 6 to page 8;

https://arxiv.org/pdf/2310.12456, Khan's notes on stacks. Key definitions: (1) simplicial sets on page 8 as well as the horn filling condition leading to infinity categories from page 8 to page 11; (2) sheaves and stacks from page 14 to 16. Key computations: (1) working out explicitly what nerves are in Remark 1.2.2. concretely; (2) working out concretely the descent condition based on totalisation criterion on pages 20 - 21.

https://terrytao.wordpress.com/books/additive-combinatorics/, Terence Tao and Van Vu's book on Additive Combinatorics, chapter 1: Key definitions are: (1) first moment method by Markov's inequality; Key lemmas are: (1) Markov's inequality; (2) union bound by linearity of expectations; (3) Borel-Cantelli lemma by probability as expectation of indicators, monotone convergence theorems, and Markov's inequality; (4) exercise on Bonferroni's inequality by inclusion and exclusion principle inductively after taking expectation of indicators and applying the union bound; (5) popularity principle by taking expectations on both sides.

https://www.math.uni-bonn.de/people/scholze/Condensed.pdf, Scholze's notes on condensed mathematics. Key definitions: (1) definition of condensed formalism on page 8, page 15 explicitly; (2) solid abelian groups in anticipation of completion of tensor products on page 32; (3) theorem 5.8 for extension to solid abelian groups on page 34; (4) analytic and preanalytic rings on pages 44 to 45. Key computations: (1) use of Stone-Cech compactification for the appropriate surjection on page 11; (2) proof of generation by compact projectives and proof of abelian categories on page 12 to 13; (3) computation of cohomology of condensed abelian group on compact Hausdorffs.

https://www.math.ias.edu/~bhatt/teaching/mat549f22/lectures.pdf, Bhatt's notes on prismatic F-gauges. Key definitions: (1) Breuil-Kisin twists for prismatic F-gauges on page 5; (2) heuristc remark for crystalline objects on page 6; (3) syntomic cohomology of Z_p on page 6; (1); Key computations: (1) algebraic de Rham complex on torus on page 11 giving analytification; (2) computing nature of Tate twists for Hodge-theory coefficients and lisse l-adic sheaf for etale coefficients on pages 4 - 5;

https://math.ucr.edu/home/baez/motives.pdf, Baez's notes on motives. Key definitions: (1) definition of motives on page 9. Key examples: (1) Weil's theorem and computation of projective line and Riemann sphere, cut into two parts which is F_q and 1, so q + 1 points in F_q on page 7.

https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.03382, Scholze's notes on Berkovich motives, this is a hard paper requiring a lot of background. Key definitions: (1) Berkovich spectrum and classification of Banach fields on page 2 and page 10; (2) definition of arc sheaves and arc topology at page 19 comparing to quasi-proetale topology; (3) definition of sheaf with transfer at page 35 to be worked at later. Key computations: (1) Dmot(X), Dmot(C) and DMot(k) in their respective chapters.
